How the tip deduction works

The One, Big, Beautiful Bill Act (2025) created a U.S. federal income tax deduction for qualified tips. Three facts carry most of it:

  • Up to $25,000 a year, 2025 through 2028. You deduct your qualified reported tips, capped at $25,000, from federal taxable income — no itemizing required, it works alongside the standard deduction. The cap and years are on the IRS's "no tax on tips" page.
  • It is a deduction, not an exemption. Your tips are still income; the deduction reduces the part of it federal income tax is figured on. That is why the savings above is deduction × your marginal rate, not the deduction itself.
  • It phases out at higher incomes. Under 26 U.S.C. §224, the deduction shrinks by $100 for each $1,000 of modified adjusted gross income above $150,000 ($300,000 on a joint return), down to zero.

That is the sketch. The full picture — Schedule 1-A, self-employment rules, what "qualified" excludes — is in our tip deduction guide.

Who qualifies

Three tests, all U.S. federal:

  • Your occupation is on the IRS list of occupations that customarily and regularly received tips before 2025 — servers, bartenders, bussers, hosts, delivery, and many hospitality and personal-service roles.
  • The tips are voluntary — cash or card tips from customers, or your tip-pool share. Mandatory service charges the house adds to the check do not count.
  • The tips were properly reported — on your W-2, a 1099, or Form 4137. Cash that was never reported cannot be deducted.

You also need a Social Security number on the return, and married taxpayers must file jointly to claim it.

What it does not change

  • Social Security and Medicare tax. The 7.65% FICA share still comes out of every reported tip dollar, same as before.
  • State income tax. This is a federal deduction; your state sets its own rules and may tax tips in full.
  • Reporting. You still report tips of $20 or more in a month to your employer, and the deduction only reaches tips that were reported. Skipping reporting to "save tax" now costs you the deduction too.
  • Your paycheck math. Tip-outs, tip pools, and your actual hourly are what they were — check yours with the tips per hour calculator and the tip-out calculator.

Keep records the IRS way

The deduction rides on your reported tip total, and that total rides on a daily record — date, cash tips, card tips, and what you tipped out. A number reconstructed in April is exactly what the record exists to prevent; a number written down the night you earned it is just a fact your preparer can use.

No tax on tips questions, answered

Is "no tax on tips" real?

Yes — as a U.S. federal income tax deduction for 2025 through 2028, capped at $25,000 a year, not a blanket exemption. Tips are still income, still reported, and still subject to Social Security and Medicare tax; the deduction reduces federal income tax only.

How do I calculate my no tax on tips?

Add up your qualified reported tips for the year, cap the total at $25,000, subtract any income phase-out, and multiply what is left by your marginal federal rate. That product is roughly the federal income tax the deduction saves; the deduction itself is the capped tip total.

  1. Total your reported tips for the year.
  2. Cap the total at $25,000.
  3. Subtract $100 per $1,000 of modified AGI over $150,000 ($300,000 joint).
  4. Multiply the remainder by your marginal federal rate.

How much will no tax on tips save me?

Multiply your qualified tips — up to the $25,000 annual cap — by your marginal federal rate. A server reporting $18,000 in tips in the 12% bracket saves about $2,160. The deduction applies to federal income tax for 2025-2028; Social Security and Medicare taxes still apply.

The real figure depends on your full return, which is why this page is an illustration and your preparer has the final word.

Do cash tips count?

Yes — cash, card, and tip-pool shares can all qualify, but only if they were properly reported on a W-2, a 1099, or Form 4137. Unreported cash never qualifies, which makes the daily tip record the whole ballgame.

What years does no tax on tips apply to?

U.S. federal tax years 2025 through 2028. Under current law the deduction expires after 2028 unless Congress extends it.

Do I still report my tips?

Yes, exactly as before — tips of $20 or more in a month go to your employer by the 10th of the following month, and the IRS still expects a daily record. The deduction happens on your tax return, not in your reporting.
