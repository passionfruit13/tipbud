How to calculate your tips per hour

Four steps, and only the first two matter if all you want is the shift number:

  1. Tips kept. Tips minus what you tipped out. Say you walked with $220 and passed $30 to the bar and bussers: $220 − $30 = $190.
  2. Tips per hour. Tips kept divided by hours worked. Over a 7-hour shift: $190 ÷ 7 = $27.14.
  3. True Rate. Add your base wage. At $7.25 an hour: $27.14 + $7.25 = $34.39 per hour — what the shift actually paid you.
  4. Projections. One shift of that pace is $240.75 ($7.25 × 7 + $190). At 4 shifts a week that is $963.00 a week, or about $48,150 a year across 50 working weeks.

The annual figure is a projection of one shift's pace, not a forecast — see the section on swings below before you quote it to a landlord.

What counts as a "good" hourly with tips

In the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' May 2023 Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, the median wage for waiters and waitresses (OES 35-3031) was $15.36 an hour — a figure that includes reported tips, not base pay alone. Half of servers earned more than that, half less.

The spread around that median is enormous. A slow lunch shift at a diner and a Saturday night at a busy steakhouse are different jobs financially, even at the same title. Big-city full-service restaurants routinely pay experienced servers $25–40 an hour all-in on good shifts; quiet weeknights anywhere can dip near base wage. So compare your True Rate to your own history first, and to the BLS median second.

One more thing your hourly does not show: taxes. Through 2028, U.S. federal law lets many tipped workers deduct reported tips from federal taxable income, up to a cap — see what that could be worth with the no tax on tips calculator or the full tip deduction guide.

Why your hourly swings shift to shift

  • Section and cover count. Tips scale with sales, and sales scale with how many tables you turn. A four-table section on a dead Tuesday cannot produce a Friday number.
  • Day and daypart. Weekend dinners usually run double a weekday lunch or more. Averaging one week hides this; averaging a month shows it.
  • Tip-outs on sales. If your house tips out on sales rather than tips, a table that stiffs you still generates a tip-out, so bad nights hit your hourly twice. The tip-out calculator shows exactly how much.
  • Season. Patio months, holidays, and local events move the whole distribution. An annual estimate from one January shift will read low; from one December shift, high.

The practical fix is sample size: log every shift for a few weeks and use the average, not the memory of your best night.

Tips per hour questions, answered

How to calculate tips per hour?

Divide your total tips by the hours you worked: $180 in tips over a six-hour shift is $30 per hour. For your true hourly rate, subtract tip-outs and add base wages first: (tips − tip-outs + hourly pay) ÷ hours worked.

The calculator above runs both versions live and calls the second one your True Rate.

Do I include tip-outs?

Yes — subtract them. Money you pass to bussers, runners, or the bar was never yours to keep, so counting it inflates your hourly. It also is not your income for tax purposes; you report the tips you keep.

How much do servers make per hour with tips?

The BLS May 2023 median for waiters and waitresses was $15.36 an hour including reported tips, but the range is wide — busy full-service houses commonly pay $25–40 an hour all-in on strong shifts, while slow shifts can land near base wage. Your own multi-week average is the number that matters.

Is $7.25 an hour normal for a server with tips?

Under the U.S. federal Fair Labor Standards Act, yes — employers may pay tipped employees a cash wage as low as $2.13 an hour, so a $7.25 base is above the federal tipped minimum. Tips must bring you to at least $7.25 total, and many states require a higher base. The DOL's tipped-minimum-wage table lists each state's rules.

Should I count cash and card tips separately?

Track them separately, total them for the hourly. Cash and card tips are both income and both belong in the calculation, but keeping the split matters for your records — card tips show up on your paycheck and W-2 automatically, while cash tips only get reported because you wrote them down.
